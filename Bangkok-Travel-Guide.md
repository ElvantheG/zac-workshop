## Neighbourhoods to Explore

### 🏛️ Rattanakosin (Old City)
The historic heart of Bangkok. Walk between the Grand Palace, Wat Pho, and Sanam Luang. Hire a tuk-tuk or boat to connect them.

### 🌙 Silom / Bangrak
Bangkok's financial district by day, entertainment strip by night. Patpong Night Market, rooftop bars, and easy BTS access.

### 🛍️ Sukhumvit
The expat hub — lined with malls (Terminal 21, EmQuartier), international restaurants, and easy MRT/BTS transit.

### 🎨 Ari & Phahon Yothin
Hip, local neighbourhood. Independent cafés, gallery spaces, and excellent Thai restaurants without tourist prices.

### 🍜 Yaowarat (Chinatown)
Best visited after dark. Seafood restaurants spill onto the streets; vendors sell fresh coconut ice cream, crispy pork, and dim sum from tiny carts.

### 🌿 Thonglor / Ekkamai
Bangkok's trendy east side. Brunch spots, craft cocktail bars, boutique hotels, and some of the city's best Japanese food.

## Temples & Culture

### Must-Visit Temples

**Wat Pho** — Home of the reclining Buddha (46m long). Also the original centre of traditional Thai massage. Book a 1-hour massage here for ~420 THB.

**Wat Phra Kaew (Grand Palace complex)** — The Emerald Buddha and Thailand's most sacred site. Dress code enforced: shoulders and knees covered. Sarongs available at the gate.

**Wat Arun** — Across the river from Wat Pho. The mosaic-tiled prang is most beautiful at golden hour. Take the cross-river ferry for 5 THB.

**Wat Saket (Golden Mount)** — Climb 318 steps to a gilded chedi with panoramic views of Bangkok's skyline and temples.

**Wat Benchamabophit (Marble Temple)** — Quieter, photogenic, best visited at dawn when monks receive alms.

**Wat Paknam Phasi Charoen (Big Buddha)** — Home to Bangkok's most photogenic giant Buddha — a stunning 69-metre emerald-green seated figure visible from outside and breathtaking from inside the temple's glass-ceilinged hall. Far fewer tourists than the Old City temples. Take the MRT to Bang Phai or a ferry along Khlong Bangkok Yai. Free entry; open daily 8 AM–6 PM.

### Canal Tours (Khlong Tours)

Bangkok was once called the "Venice of the East" — its network of canals (*khlongs*) is still the best way to see the quieter, residential side of the city.

#### Longtail Boat Charter (~1,000–1,500 THB for 1–2 hours)
Hire a longtail from Tha Chang or Tha Tien pier (near Wat Pho) for a private canal tour. A good route covers:
- **Khlong Bangkok Noi** — Passes the Royal Barges National Museum (worth a quick stop)
- **Khlong Bangkok Yai** — Lined with wooden houses, orchid farms, and small riverside temples
- **Khlong Mon** — The most scenic stretch; locals fish from stilted houses

> 💡 **Tip:** Negotiate the route and price before boarding. Ask to stop at **Wat Arun** on the return — the river approach is the most dramatic angle.

#### Khlong Saen Saep Express Boat (15–22 THB per hop)
The local commuter canal cuts east–west through the city. Cheap, fast, and very local. Board near Central World (Pratunam pier) heading east toward Ramkhamhaeng, or west toward the Old City.

#### Guided Canal & Community Tours (~800–1,200 THB)
Several operators run small-group kayak or slow-boat tours through Thonburi's backwater canals, stopping at:
- Floating markets and roadside shrines
- Traditional coconut sugar and Thai dessert workshops
- Community orchid and tropical plant gardens

**Recommended operator:** Bangkok Canal Tour boats from Tha Maharaj pier. Book via Klook or direct at the pier.

### Cultural Experiences

- **Thai Cooking Class** — Half-day classes at Blue Elephant or smaller local schools; ~1,500–2,500 THB. Start with a market visit.
- **Traditional Thai Massage** — Avoid tourist traps; go to Wat Pho school or Ruen-Nuad Massage Studio in Silom.
- **Muay Thai** — Watch a live bout at Rajadamnern or Lumpinee Stadium. Ringside seats are worth it.
- **Monk Chat at Wat Mahathat** — Free, informal conversations with monks learning English. Genuinely fascinating.

## Markets & Shopping

### Weekend Markets
- **Chatuchak Weekend Market** — 15,000+ stalls; one of the world's largest markets. Saturday & Sunday, 9 AM–6 PM. Sections for vintage clothing, plants, art, street food. Take the BTS to Mo Chit.
- **JJ Green Night Market** — Near Chatuchak; vintage and retro goods in an atmospheric outdoor setting.

### Night Markets
- **Jodd Fairs (Dan Neramit)** — Bangkok's trendiest current night market. Neon lights, street food, indie fashion. Open Thu–Sun.
- **Asiatique The Riverfront** — Open-air mall along the Chao Phraya with boutiques, restaurants, and a Ferris wheel.
- **Talad Neon (Pratunam Night Market)** — Fashion-forward market near Pratunam; great for cheap clothing.

### Malls (When You Need AC)
- **Terminal 21** — Each floor themed as a global city; brilliant food court on the top floor (Pier 21).
- **ICON Siam** — Luxury riverside mall with an indoor floating market. Worth visiting just for the spectacle.
- **Siam Paragon / CentralWorld** — Bangkok's classic upscale shopping corridor.

### Restaurants Worth the Sit-Down
- **Jay Fai** ⭐ — Michelin-starred street food. Crab omelette for ~1,000 THB. Book weeks ahead (or arrive at opening).
- **Nahm** — Modern Thai fine dining at Como Metropolitan.
- **Gaggan Anand** — Avant-garde Indian cuisine; consistently ranks in Asia's 50 Best.
- **Baan Ying** — Local favourite for comfort Thai food; multiple locations.
- **Raan Jay Fai** (budget version) — If Jay Fai's queue is impossible, nearby street stalls on Maha Chai Road do similar dishes.

## Nightlife & Bars

### Rooftop Bars
- **Vertigo & Moon Bar (Banyan Tree Hotel)** — Open-air, 61 floors up. Dress smart-casual.
- **Sky Bar at Lebua (State Tower)** — The "Hangover 2" bar. Arrive before sunset for golden views.
- **Three Sixty (Millennium Hilton)** — More relaxed vibe, great Chao Phraya views.

### Craft Cocktail Bars
- **Rabbit Hole** (Thonglor) — Industrial-chic bar with serious cocktail programme.
- **Teens of Thailand** (Chinatown) — Gin bar hidden inside Chinatown; must-visit.
- **Backstage Cocktail Bar** (Pathumwan) — Theatre-themed, creative menus.

### Nightclubs & Music
- **Onyx** (Royal City Avenue / RCA) — Bangkok's largest club; big-name DJs.
- **Levels Club** (Sukhumvit Soi 11) — Popular with expats and tourists, multiple floors.
- **Studio Lam** (Thonglor) — Bangkok's best indie/alternative DJ bar; world music and funk.

## Getting Around

### BTS Skytrain & MRT
The fastest and cheapest way to cross the city. Runs 6 AM–midnight. Single journeys from ~17 THB. Get a **Rabbit card** (BTS) for tap-and-go.

### Chao Phraya Express Boat
Hop on at any pier for 15–40 THB per trip. The tourist boat (orange flag) hits all major sights for a flat 150 THB/day.

### Tuk-Tuk
Fun for short hops — always agree on the price before you get in. Good rule: anything over 1 km, negotiate hard or take a metered taxi.

### Grab (Ride-Hailing)
The Thai equivalent of Uber. Works seamlessly; pricing is fixed before booking. Download before you arrive.

### Metered Taxis
Make sure the driver uses the meter. If they refuse, find another cab. Base fare starts at 35 THB.

### Motorcycle Taxis
Orange-vested drivers at every corner. Fast and cheap for final-mile trips in traffic (20–50 THB). Helmets available — use them.

## Practical Tips

### Best Time to Visit
- **Cool & dry season (Nov–Feb):** Best weather. Expect crowds.
- **Hot season (Mar–May):** 38°C+. Stay hydrated; malls are a refuge.
- **Rainy season (Jun–Oct):** Short, heavy downpours in the afternoon. Prices drop; fewer tourists.

### Money
- ATMs are everywhere but charge 200–220 THB foreign transaction fee per withdrawal. Withdraw larger amounts less frequently.
- Street food is cash-only. Carry small bills (20, 50, 100 THB).
- Currency: Thai Baht (THB). 1 USD ≈ 35 THB.

### Health & Safety
- Drink bottled or filtered water only — tap water is not safe for tourists.
- Bangkok is very safe for tourists. Common scam: tuk-tuk driver offers cheap city tour but takes you to gem shops (say no thanks).
- Pharmacies (Boots, Watsons) are ubiquitous for any minor medical needs.
- Travel insurance is strongly recommended.

### Cultural Etiquette
- 🙏 **Wai greeting** — Press palms together and bow slightly when meeting someone; return wais directed at you.
- 👑 **The monarchy is sacred** — Never make negative comments about the royal family.
- 🛕 **Temple dress code** — Cover shoulders and knees. Remove shoes before entering temple buildings.
- 🤫 **Volume** — Thais value calm and "saving face." Raising your voice in frustration will not help you.

### Useful Apps
- **Grab** — Taxis and food delivery
- **Google Maps** — Works well; includes BTS/MRT routes
- **Klook** — Book activities and attractions in advance
- **Google Translate (camera mode)** — Essential for menus and signs in Thai script
